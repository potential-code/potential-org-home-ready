A1 reading – Your real budget (Dubai). The rent in an advert is only part of what you will pay in your first year. Before you commit, add up five things. First, the annual rent and how you will pay it: landlords often accept 1 to 4 cheques; fewer cheques can lower the rent but need more cash up front. Second, the security deposit, commonly about 5% of the annual rent for an unfurnished home and 10% for a furnished one; it is refundable if you leave the home in good condition. Third, the agency fee if an agent is involved, commonly around 5% of the annual rent. Fourth, registration and connection costs: the tenancy contract must be registered in Ejari, and you will open a DEWA account for electricity and water, which needs a refundable deposit. Fifth, running costs: DEWA bills, district cooling (chiller) charges in some buildings, internet, and the Dubai Municipality housing fee that appears on your DEWA bill. Put these into the true-cost calculator and compare homes on their full yearly cost, not on the advertised rent. Checked on 25 Nov 2026 · Sources: Dubai Land Department (Ejari), DEWA. Typical percentages are market practice, not fixed rules. Education, not legal or financial advice.
A2 reading – Choosing the area. Start with the things you cannot change easily: where you work and, if you have children, where they will study. Measure the commute at the time you would really travel, by car and by metro if it is an option. Then list the services you use every week: supermarkets, clinics, gyms, mosques or places of worship, parks. Check parking: how many spaces come with the home and whether visitor parking exists. Think about noise and construction nearby; a quiet street today may be next to a building site next year. Finally, compare rents in two or three areas that meet your needs using the DLD Rental Index, so you know the typical range before you negotiate. Activity: drag your priorities (commute, schools, budget, lifestyle, space) into order; Home Ready saves them to your profile and uses them in the decision journey. Checked on 25 Nov 2026 · Source: Dubai Land Department Rental Index. Education, not advice.
A3 reading – The viewing. Treat a viewing like an inspection. Open every tap and check water pressure and hot water. Run the air conditioning in every room and ask when it was last serviced. Look for damp, mould or stains on ceilings and around windows. Test windows, doors and locks. Check the kitchen appliances that are included and write them down. Ask who pays for maintenance and how quickly repairs are handled. Ask whether cooling is included or billed separately, and by whom. Ask how many cheques the landlord accepts and whether the rent changes with fewer cheques. Ask to see the title deed or proof that the person renting the home has the right to do so, and check the agent's RERA registration. Take photos and notes, and save them in your viewing checklist so you can compare homes later. Checked on 25 Nov 2026 · Source: RERA broker registry (Dubai Land Department). Education, not advice.
A4 reading – The contract and renewal (Dubai). In Dubai the tenancy contract must be registered in Ejari; you need it for DEWA, residence matters and any dispute. Read the contract before you sign: the rent, payment dates, deposit, maintenance responsibilities and any additional terms. At renewal, either side that wants to change the terms, including the rent, must give the other written notice at least 90 days before the contract ends. Rent increases are linked to the RERA Rental Index: if your rent is already close to the average for similar homes in your area, the landlord cannot increase it, and the allowed increase rises in steps the further below the average your rent is. A landlord who wants the home back for personal use or sale must give 12 months' notice through a notary public or registered mail. If you disagree, the Rental Disputes Settlement Centre handles tenancy disputes. Before your renewal, run the Rental Index check and the Renew-or-move journey. Checked on 25 Nov 2026 · Sources: Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007 and No. 33 of 2008; Decree No. 43 of 2013; Dubai Land Department. Education, not legal advice.
A5 reading – Moving in and moving out. On the day you get the keys, walk through the home with the landlord or agent and record its condition room by room, with dated photos and a signed condition report. Note anything that is broken or worn, even if it seems small. Keep copies of the contract, Ejari certificate, cheque receipts and any repair requests. When you plan to leave, give notice as your contract requires, arrange a final inspection, and compare the home with your move-in report. Normal wear and tear should not be deducted from your deposit; damage beyond that may be. Settle final DEWA and cooling bills and close the accounts to recover their deposits. Ask for your security deposit to be returned in writing with any deductions explained. Checked on 25 Nov 2026 · Source: Dubai Land Department. Education, not advice.
